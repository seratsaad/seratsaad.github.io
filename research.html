🔭 My Research

🌌 Wide Binaries & Modified Gravity (MOND)

At The Ohio State University, I am working on wide binary stars as precision tools to test gravitational physics in the low-acceleration regime. My current research explores how their orbital dynamics can be used to probe deviations from Newtonian gravity under the framework Modified Newtonian Dynamics (MOND), combining Gaia astrometry with high-resolution spectroscopic radial velocities data from Keck, VLT, Magellan, and LBTT telescopes. I'll update my progress on this project soon.

🌟 Young Stars & Stellar Formation

  • ABYSS II: Identifying Young Stars in Optical SDSS Spectra
    Developed a full data pipeline to detect and characterize young stellar objects (YSOs) in SDSS-BOSS spectra, measuring veiling and accretion properties. Published as a first-author paper in The Astronomical Journal.
  • Empirical Veiling Measurements in BOSS Spectra
    Built a robust empirical calibration for stellar veiling in optical spectra, validating accretion diagnostics for hundreds of YSOs. Published in The Astronomical Journal and presented at multiple conferences.

🌀 Active Galactic Nuclei & Black Hole Binaries

  • Tick Tock: Spectroscopic Analysis of J1430
    Investigating periodic optical variability in the quasar J1430+2303 using UV–optical spectra from the Hubble Space Telescope and ground-based facilities. Proposed an alternative explanation to the supermassive black hole binary hypothesis — manuscript in preparation for The Astrophysical Journal.
  • J0950+5128: Perturbed Accretion Disk Dynamics
    Performing line-profile decomposition and disk modeling to understand deviations from standard AGN accretion behavior. Aiming to constrain disk kinematics and binary-induced perturbations.
